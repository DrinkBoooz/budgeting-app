Starting your first job

Under the First Time Jobseekers Assistance Act, government offices waive the fees once for: police and NBI clearance, barangay clearance, a medical certificate from a public hospital, PSA birth and marriage certificates, a transcript from a state school, your TIN and your UMID card. Get a barangay certification that you're a first-time jobseeker first. It's good for a year.

Your first employer registers you with BIR, and the TIN you get is yours for life: give the same one to every employer after. Having two is against the law, with a fine of up to ₱1,000, up to six months in jail, or both. If you already have one from school or a business permit, use that.

It usually covers only the days since you started, not a full cutoff. Some companies also pay new hires one payday late while they set you up, and a month's contributions may come off a short paycheck. From the second full cutoff, it should match what you see here.
